## 1. WHAT IT IS DOING

**Book 1 — Leonard (live, real money).** Buys high-probability weather
contracts (85–98c favorites, mostly NO on daily high/low temperature
strikes), then rests a time-decaying sell ladder above them and lifts
the inventory back out before settlement. It never forecasts anything
it doesn't have to: the edge is that a temperature market converges
toward a knowable number all day, so inventory bought at 88c walks up
into a 96–99c bid. Risk is bounded by seven caps (per bet, per city,
per slate, per metric-slate, open, daily, weekly).

**Book 4 — Phantom (paper, zero dollars).** A market-making desk on
MLB and tennis. It rests two-sided quotes across hundreds of markets
and fills ONLY when a real print trades through the price it was
resting at. Three lanes: `tight` (join the touch of a 1–3c book),
`wide` (step inside a ≥4c book), and `fav` (80–95c favorites — not
market-making at all, but Leonard's shape ported onto sports).

## 4. WHAT WE ARE LEARNING

1. **The exit is the strategy.** Two days, two autopsies, same answer:
   this book's P&L is decided by how positions END, not how they start.
   Lift +$0.203, settle −$0.657.
2. **Kalshi's fee is a parabola peaking at 50c.** Everything we have
   ever made is at 85–98c, where the maker fee is 0.08–0.28c. The paper
   book lost money mostly because we built it at the peak.
3. **Never apply a round-trip mechanism to a hold-to-drift trade.** The
   same shove that recycles capital in a making lane destroys the fav
   lane. Mechanisms must be lane-aware.
4. **A lane with no quote budget is a lane with no evidence** — we
   spent a full day disproving the cheap lane while never sampling the
   expensive one.
5. **Volume is not the business; turnover × edge is.** We reached
   ~8x capital turnover per hour and it made the losses arrive faster.
6. **Silent counters lie.** ovn_lo_blocked read `null`, `count_fp` was
   the real field, quotes were filled by past prints. Every one was
   found by asking "is this number real?" rather than by a test.
