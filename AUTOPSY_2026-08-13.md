## 3. The structural problem (read this twice)

The core lane buys **favorites at 80–99¢ and holds to settlement**. At 88¢ you risk 88 to
win 12. The break-even win rate is ~88%.

**Our actual win rate is 78%** (261 W / 73 L).

That gap is the whole story. 261 wins and 73 losses still equals **−$83** because each loss
erases roughly seven wins. This is why the equity curve grinds up and then gives it all back
in one night — and it means **more volume makes it worse, not better.** A negative-expectancy
lane cannot compound. There is no sizing, no boost, no capital injection that converts a
−EV trade into growth; leverage only accelerates the arrival at zero.

The bot's own calibration table says the same thing more politely: in every probability
bucket, *actual* outcomes land below what the model predicted (e.g. model 86.7% vs actual
81.8% in the top band; model 30.5% vs actual 8.8% in the 20–40% band). **The model is
systematically overconfident.** We have been paying 88¢ for outcomes worth about 82¢.

## 6. What to change — ranked by expected impact

### Tier 1 — do before another dollar is risked
1. **Re-anchor the dashboard to cash in.** Deposits, withdrawals, realized, unrealized,
   true % return. No more steering by a broken gauge.
2. **Stop holding favorites into settlement.** Mandatory flatten: any position still open
   T-minus N minutes before close gets sold at market. This converts the fat left tail

3. **Per-market hard dollar stop, enforced at the position level** — no single market may
   ever exceed the bet cap, regardless of pyramiding, adoption lag, or re-entry.

### Tier 2 — make the winning engine the main engine
4. **Promote the offer/recycle lane to primary**: enter only where we can quote both sides,
   size for round trips, measure P&L per round trip rather than per settlement.
5. **Re-validate the entry model against its own calibration table** — it is overconfident
   in every bucket. Either recalibrate (shrink probabilities toward market) or only trade
   where *market* price is below *actual* frequency, which the table says is the 0–20% band
   (sell rich longshots) rather than the 80–99% band we've been buying.
6. **Weekly circuit breaker** (−15% week = stop, human review).

### Tier 3 — only after expectancy is proven positive on Kalshi's ledger
7. Breadth: more cities/series, sports book if it can be made to work.
8. Capital. **Not before.** Adding money to a −EV system is how a $200 loss becomes a
   $2,000 loss.

## 7. On "going parabolic"

I want to be straight with you, because I think it matters more than telling you what
would be pleasant to hear.

Compounding only works when expectancy per trade is positive. Right now it is negative, and
the honest read of 334 settlements is that the entry model does not have the edge we thought
it had. The fastest route to growth is therefore not more volume, more size, or more capital
— it is **removing the losing lane and letting the one profitable engine (spread capture with
pre-settlement exits) run**.

The second thing worth saying plainly: yesterday's push for speed is what produced a
33%-of-NAV single-city position, and that position cost more than a month of gains. When the
goal becomes "parabolic," risk controls start looking like obstacles. They are the only
reason this account is at $98 instead of $0.

A realistic, genuinely good outcome for this system: a small, verifiable edge on a few
hundred dollars, compounding slowly, with drawdowns bounded by rules we don't override.
That's not a consolation prize — it's the only version of this that survives long enough
to matter.

**My recommendation: pause live entries, run the fixed strategy in paper for a defined
sample, and go back live only when Kalshi's own settlement ledger shows positive
expectancy.** The plumbing is finally sound. Let's point it at a trade that actually wins.
